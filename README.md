Spatially Differentiated Conservation and Management Strategies for Coastal Blue Carbon Ecosystems in China

1. Dataset description

This dataset contains the spatially explicit conservation and management strategies presented in Fig. 4, “Spatially differentiated conservation and management strategies” of the associated study:

Xu, Y., Gu, T., Xu, F., et al. Dual-constraint spatial planning unlocks proactive blue carbon expansion across China’s coastlines.

The dataset provides the final spatial allocation of conservation and management strategies for three coastal blue carbon ecosystems (CBCEs): mangroves, salt marshes, and seagrass, together with areas prioritized for anthropogenic activities and other areas.

The data are provided in ESRI Shapefile format (.shp). The spatial planning framework consists of 397,961 planning units at a spatial resolution of 30 arc-seconds. The dataset should be used together with all standard Shapefile components, including .shp, .shx, .dbf, and .prj. Each planning unit is assigned a unique identification number and one categorical strategy code describing its corresponding conservation or management strategy.

2. Spatial planning framework

The final spatial strategy was developed using a systematic conservation planning framework integrating ecosystem suitability, existing CBCE distributions, protection priorities, and anthropogenic pressure. Based on the comparative assessment of alternative planning solutions, a potential distribution probability threshold of >0.4 and the R2 frequency solution were selected for the final conservation planning scheme (The R2 frequency solution represents planning units selected in 60–100% of 100 independent Marxan iterations.). A 30% protection target was subsequently applied to formulate the comprehensive spatial protection strategy. The resulting strategy provides a spatially differentiated framework for the protection, restoration, and management of CBCEs.

4. Interpretation of the dataset

The strategy code assigned to each planning unit represents the recommended spatial conservation or management strategy generated by the study, rather than an existing legal designation or officially established protected-area boundary. In particular:
Codes 11–16 identify mangrove-related conservation strategies.
Codes 21–26 identify salt-marsh-related conservation strategies.
Codes 31–36 identify seagrass-related conservation strategies.
Codes 41–43 identify areas prioritized for anthropogenic activities.
Code 50 identifies other areas.

For mangrove, salt marsh, and seagrass categories, the second digit further distinguishes the strategy and protection status.

5. Data limitations and appropriate use

a. The planning units are analytical units used for systematic conservation planning and do not represent administrative boundaries or legally designated protected areas.
b. The spatial resolution of the dataset is 30 arc-seconds.
c. The final strategy was developed under a 30% protection target and therefore represents a specific conservation planning scenario.
d. The proposed strategies should be regarded as a research-based spatial decision-support framework, rather than statutory land-use or marine spatial-planning boundaries.
e. Local implementation should consider site-specific ecological, socioeconomic, and policy conditions.
